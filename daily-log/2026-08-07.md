## ✅ What I covered

- **The core reframe:** you get handed a *solution* ("build us an AI agent") and your first job is to recover the *problem*.
- **The five value pillars** — efficiency, transformation, productivity, cost, performance/SLAs — and why the pillar changes the correct architecture for the same problem.
- **The translation ladder** (five rungs, in order).
- Worked example: claims first-notice-of-loss review → the "agent" ask resolved to a **workflow with a routing step**.
- The two question shapes this domain uses, and their trap answers.

## 💡 Key takeaways (in my own words)

1. **The value pillar determines what "success" means.** A *cost* play dies if the architecture costs more than the humans; a *performance* play dies at p95; a *transformation* play can justify Opus and 10-second latency because nobody was doing the work at all before.

2. **The translation ladder:** business outcome + baseline → success metric **and cost of being wrong** → hard constraints → simplest architecture that clears them → feedback loop. Skipping a rung is how you end up with an agent nobody needed.

3. **Failure cost does most of the design work, and it's often asymmetric.** In the claims example, a wrongly auto-*rejected* claim is a regulatory complaint while a wrongly auto-*approved* one is recoverable — so: auto-approve freely, never auto-reject. That asymmetry produced the architecture almost by itself.

4. **Trap answers:** in "which architecture fits?", the trap is the most autonomous option. In "what do you do first?", the trap is any option that starts designing.

5. **Two habits that earn points on every design answer:** name the **trade-off** accepted, and name **what would change your mind**.
