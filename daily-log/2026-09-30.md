## 💡 Key takeaways (in my own words)

1. **Roles:** the **host** is the user-facing application (Claude Code, Claude Desktop, your app). It spawns one **client** per server, and each client holds a **1:1 stateful session** with one **server** that exposes capabilities. One host, many clients, many servers — the client is the per-connection object, not a separate program.

2. **The three primitives, by controller** — this is the most commonly tested detail:

   | Primitive | Controlled by | Meaning |
   | --------- | ------------- | ------- |
   | **Tools** | **Model** | Claude decides to invoke them |
   | **Resources** | **Application / host** | The host attaches them as context, addressed by URI |
   | **Prompts** | **User** | The user explicitly picks them (e.g. a slash command) |

3. **Transports:** **stdio** for local, single-user, same-machine servers — a subprocess, no network, nothing to authenticate. **Streamable HTTP** for remote, shared, or multi-user — and the moment you go remote you own **OAuth, tenant isolation, scaling, and network failure modes**.

4. **Tool results are untrusted input.** A document returned by a server can carry injected instructions. Never let a tool result authorise a privileged action on its own. (Thread to Domain 5, Day 25.)

5. **MCP standardises the *interface*, not the trust.** It gives a common schema, discovery, and transport contract. It does not give you authorization, entitlement filtering, or input sanitisation — those remain your design problem (Day 13).
